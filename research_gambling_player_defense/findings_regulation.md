# Findings — gambling regulation that affects online casino players

Scope: player-protection duties and gaps, not operator-licensing trivia. Research date: 2026-10-13. Not legal advice. No diagnostic claims.

---

## 1. United Kingdom (UK Gambling Commission)

UK-licensed remote operators owe players a licence-condition duty of care. It is not a vague vibe. LCCP Social Responsibility Code 3.4.3 requires operators to **identify, act, and evaluate** harm risk from the moment an account opens, using spend, patterns of spend, time, behaviour, customer-led contact, use of gambling-management tools, and account indicators.

> "Licensees must implement effective customer interaction systems and processes in a way which minimises the risk of customers experiencing harms associated with gambling."
>
> Source: [LCCP 3.4.3 Remote customer interaction](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/lccp/condition/3-4-3-remote-customer-interaction)

Action must escalate. Operators must cut or block marketing and new bonus offers when harm indicators appear, and must **prevent** marketing and new bonuses when **strong** indicators appear. They can end the business relationship. Strong-indicator action must be automated, then manually reviewed, and the player can contest the automated decision.

### Deposit / loss / session limits

Operators already had to offer personal budget tools at registration or first deposit. The Commission then made the tools less weaselly:

From **31 October 2025** (Remote Technical Standards, phase 1):

- Prompt the player to set a financial limit **before the first deposit**.
- Free-text account-level limits (not just preset buttons).
- Limit-setting link on homepage and deposit pages, few clicks.
- Decrease a limit **immediately**.
- Remind the player every **six months** to review account and transaction information.

From **30 September 2026** (phase 2, extended from 30 June 2026):

- Operators must offer a **gross deposit limit** (money paid in over a set duration).
- Only that product may be called a "deposit limit".
- Gross deposit limits must have at least equal prominence to loss or net-style limits.
- Other limit types (loss, net of withdrawals) remain optional extras.

> "From 30 June 2026 all online operators must provide customers with the opportunity to set a ‘deposit limit’ which is based solely on the amount a customer pays into their account over a set duration. To avoid confusion, only this form of limit may be called a ‘deposit limit’."
>
> Source: [New deposit limit rules provide clarity for consumers](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/new-deposit-limit-rules-provide-clarity-for-consumers) (implementation later extended to 30 September 2026: [implementation extension](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/implementation-extension-for-new-deposit-limit-requirements))

Session intensity is also regulated. From 17 January 2025, remote game-design rules ban autoplay, turbo / slam-stop, celebrations of returns at or below stake, operator-led multi-table play, and casino spin speeds under 5 seconds (peer-to-peer poker excepted). Operators must show **net spend and time spent gambling in real time**.

Source: [New rules boosting safety and consumer choice](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/new-rules-boosting-safety-and-consumer-choice)

### Affordability / financial-risk checks

Two layers:

1. **Light-touch financial vulnerability checks** (live): public-data checks when net deposits exceed £150 in 30 days (phased in at £500 from 30 August 2024, then £150 from 28 February 2025). Aimed at bankruptcy / unpaid-debt flags. Not a full income interrogation. Does not use postcode or job title. Does not affect credit scores.

2. **Financial risk assessments** (phased, not a blanket affordability quiz): first stage targets unusually high spend — typically **£5,000 net deposit in a rolling 24 hours** at the largest operators. Later intended thresholds (age 25+): £1,000 / 24h or £3,000 / 90 days; under 25: £750 / 24h or £2,000 / 90 days. Operators get a risk score plus defaults, multiple arrears, significant arrears, and Debt Management Plan flags. They are expected to take proportionate action (limits, less marketing, or more), not auto-ban on a missed phone bill.

Source: [New rules boosting safety and consumer choice](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/new-rules-boosting-safety-and-consumer-choice); [Financial risk assessments update – July 2026](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/blog/post/financial-risk-assessments-update-july-2026)

### GAMSTOP (multi-operator self-exclusion)

Since 31 March 2020, every GB remote licensee must participate. Periods: six months, one year, five years, or five years with auto-renewal. It is free. It is **not** a worldwide block.

> "GAMSTOP can only assist to exclude you from engaging in gambling services offered by the participating Gambling Operators. We are unable to help prevent you from accessing gambling services that are operated by organisations which do not participate in GAMSTOP."
>
> Source: [GAMSTOP Terms of Use](https://www.about.gamstop.co.uk/terms-of-use) (updated 20 May 2026)

Crypto / offshore / "not on Gamstop" brands sit outside this list by design.

### Bonus advertising and marketing

Player-facing rules that matter:

- Strong harm indicators: **no marketing, no new bonus offers** (LCCP 3.4.3 paras 9–10).
- From 1 May 2025, online operators must let customers opt in by **product type** and **channel**. Unwanted bonus spam is a compliance failure, not a growth tactic.
- Game-design rules above also kill "you are due" celebration noise on losing spins.

### Withdrawal expectations

UK sources in this pass treat reverse withdrawals and delayed cash-out as **harm markers** and fair-treatment issues, not as a published statutory SLA (for example "payout in 24 hours"). Commission case examples used to justify financial checks include operators letting a customer lose £70,000 in 10 hours on day two, or setting a £1,300 monthly deposit cap against £1,400 monthly income. The player expectation in a UK-licensed shop: you can set a deposit limit, you can take a win out without being farmed through bonus loops, and the operator can be complained about to the Commission / ADR. That stack does not travel with you to an unlicensed site.

### Duty of care, in one line

UK duty of care is operator-side and licence-backed: monitor, intervene, cut bonuses, cut marketing, close the account if needed. It is **not** a third-party warranty that the player cannot still open a Curaçao tab.

## 2. Malta Gaming Authority / EU-ish standard

MGA Player Protection Directive 2 of 2018 is the EU hub rulebook. It is real, but **operator-led**, not UK-style centralised.

Mandatory tools:

- Self-exclusion, definite or indefinite, **immediate**, no "stay and spin" pitch.
- **Either** deposit limits **or** wagering limits (both encouraged; loss and session/time limits encouraged, not mandatory).
- Reality checks: timed pop-up that pauses play and shows time, wagered, win/loss. Player must confirm and can end the session.
- Responsible-gaming page **one click** from anywhere.
- ADR in the EU/EEA; ADR outcome binding; player can still go to court.

> "A request for a new self-exclusion, or extending a current exclusion must be implemented immediately, with no attempts to induce a player to continue playing."
>
> "During a self-exclusion, players must not access gaming services, however, players must not be prevented from withdrawing funds."
>
> Source: [MGA Player Protection](https://www.mga.org.mt/licensee-hub/compliance/player-protection/)

Tighten a limit: immediate. Loosen a limit: **at least 24 hours**. Cross-brand exclusion is required when there are "sufficient indications" of problem gambling; otherwise a multi-brand group may exclude one brand only. Reopen after definite exclusion: 24-hour cool-off recommended; after indefinite: 7 days, plus an explanation that the player can now manage play.

Harm markers operators must watch (minimum): deposit/wager amount and frequency, multiple payment methods, **reversal of pending withdrawals**, complaint/bonus-request spikes, use of RG tools.

What Malta does **not** give UK players:

- No GAMSTOP-equivalent national multi-operator register.
- No UK-style financial-vulnerability / credit-reference risk assessments.
- No product bans as tight as UK autoplay / turbo / 5-second spin rules (MGA writes the framework; the operator evidences the controls).

Source: [MGA Player Protection](https://www.mga.org.mt/licensee-hub/compliance/player-protection/); [MGA FAQ — mandatory RG tools](https://www.mga.org.mt/faqs/remote-gaming-are-there-any-mandatory-responsible-gambling-tools-which-must-be-offered-to-players/); [MGA Self-Exclusion Thematic Review (PDF)](https://www.mga.org.mt/app/uploads/Thematic-Review-Self-Exclusion-Practices.pdf)

## 3. United States — state lists, not a national net

There is no federal online-casino self-exclusion list. Coverage is **state-licensed product only**.

### New Jersey (primary example)

One DGE request covers **all New Jersey internet gaming and online sports wagering**. Online terms: **one year or five years**; name stays on the list until the player later asks for removal after the minimum term. Lifetime requires in-person or video conference. Caught playing while excluded: **forfeit winnings and related account funds**. Operators may also ban the person from land-based and out-of-state sister properties and strip rewards.

> "Only one request for Self-Exclusion is required to self-exclude from all New Jersey Internet gaming and online sports wagering sites."
>
> "It is your responsibility to refrain from gaming activities. DGE, the casinos and the Internet gaming platform providers are not liable for any acts or omissions in processing or enforcing of your request for self-exclusion..."
>
> Source: [NJ DGE Self-Exclusion Instructions](https://www.njportal.com/DGE/SelfExclusion/OptOut/Instructions) (Casino Control Act N.J.S.A. 5:12-71.2 / 71.3; N.J.A.C. 13:69G-2.1–2.5)

### New York and Massachusetts (same pattern)

- **NY:** one Commission form can cover sports wagering, commercial casinos, lottery products, IFS, charitable gaming, and other Commission-regulated activity. Operators must refuse new deposits, stop marketing, and deny comps. Terms: 1 / 3 / 5 years; auto-remove unless renewed within 30 days. Source: [NYS Gaming Commission — Voluntary Self-Exclusion](https://gaming.ny.gov/voluntary-self-exclusion)
- **MA:** Voluntary Self-Exclusion can be gaming-only, sports-only, or both. Covers MA casinos, sportsbooks, and **in-state** sports-wagering platforms. Rewards forfeited. Operators may share the list with out-of-state sister properties. Source: [MGC VSE brochure 2025 (PDF)](https://massgaming.com/wp-content/uploads/MGC-VSE-Brochue-2025.pdf)

### The offshore / crypto gap

State lists do not bind unlicensed, sweepstakes, or crypto-cashier sites. Those operators are not on the DGE / NYSGC / MGC feed and have no legal duty to honour it. GAMSTOP is UK-only. A New Jersey exclusion does not close a Curaçao tab or a Telegram dice bot.

> "Offshore or unlicensed gambling — No state program should be assumed to cover it."
>
> Source: [The Playbook USA — state programs](https://theplaybookusa.com/playbook/basics/responsible/state-programs/); [What self-exclusion blocks and misses](https://theplaybookusa.com/playbook/basics/responsible/self-exclusion/)

## 4. Curaçao / grey market — what the player does **not** get

Post-LOK Curaçao (CGA) now writes operator-level RG policy: age checks, deposit limits (daily/weekly/monthly), cooling-off, self-exclusion, behaviour tracking, marketing controls. Tighten a deposit limit immediately; loosen after 24 hours. That is paper progress.

What a player still does **not** get:

- **No central self-exclusion register.** Exclude at Brand A, Brand B can still take the deposit. No GAMSTOP, no NJ DGE list.
- **No individual complaint court at the regulator.** "The CGA does not handle individual complaints — it is not a civil court." Licence revocation is a systemic stick. It does not refund *you*.
- **No UK/MGA-grade ADR default** that the player can actually reach from a VPN signup.
- **No financial-vulnerability or affordability check** tied to public credit data.
- **No extra-territorial enforcement** in the player's home state. A Curaçao licence is not permission to serve the UK, US, NL, FR, AU, etc. Operators are supposed to geoblock those markets. Grey-market sites that still take UK/US players are offering **unlicensed** play there. The player has no home-regulator complaint path.
- **No guaranteed withdrawal clock, no bonus-advertising code with teeth, no duty to stop marketing a tilting customer.**

Sources: [CGA Responsible Gaming Policy (PDF)](https://portal.gamingcontrolcuracao.org/uploads/publications/njQTNnLQ58QstzkXO5DB3XC0gE3aDeC29jpLcQLW.pdf); [Curaçao post-LOK self-exclusion](https://gamingcompliance.io/cura-ao-post-lok-what-operator-level-self-exclusion-now-actually-demands-under-the-cga/); [Curaçao Gaming Licence Guide (player-limitation language)](https://casinocuracao.co.uk/curacao-gaming-license-guide); [Curaçao licence geographic limits](https://vantegris.com/blog/curacao-gaming-licence-countries/)

## 5. What a third-party player tool can do without being a licensed operator

**Legal lane (consumer software / information, not a casino):**

- Device or browser **blocking** of gambling URLs and apps (Gamban, BetBlocker). Regulators already point people at these because GAMSTOP / state lists stop at the licensed perimeter. Blocking unlicensed sites is the point, not a licence trigger.
- Player-set **friction** (spin delay, session cover, cooldown) on the player's own device.
- **Public trust scores**, RTP, payout evidence, licence status — editorial / consumer information. Advisory. Not a block. Not a warranty.
- **On-chain self-lock of the player's own keys / funds**, signed by the player, no third-party custody, no operator settlement. The casino does not have to honour it unless it chooses to. That is a player control, not "providing facilities for gambling."
- Signposting official schemes (GAMSTOP, NJ DGE, NY VSE, MA VSE) and help orgs. Do not impersonate them.

GAMSTOP itself is a statutory-condition scheme for licensees, not a gambling operator. Gamban states it is independent of the commercial gambling industry and blocks regulated **and** black-market apps.

> "Both regulated and black market gambling apps and websites won’t be accessible when you install Gamban."
>
> Source: [Why regulators choose Gamban](https://gamban.com/announcements/why-regulators-choose-gamban)

BetBlocker is explicit that national schemes only reach in-country licensees, so the blocker exists to cover the rest.

Source: [BetBlocker FAQ](https://betblocker.org/); [Gamban EULA](https://gamban.com/eula) (software "will not cure" anything; it creates friction; VPN can weaken it)

**Do not cross into operator territory:**

- Accept bets, hold stakes, run games, or settle wins/losses.
- Take custody of player deposits or withdrawal rails (that is cashier / e-money / gambling-facility space).
- Sell or broker casino accounts, bonus abuse, or geo-spoof access.
- Claim to be a regulator, a GAMSTOP equivalent, a licensed operator, or a medical / diagnostic service.
- Guarantee that exclusion or a trust score will stop play. Official schemes already refuse that guarantee.
- Scrape or mine GAMSTOP (their terms forbid it).

Honesty rule that matches the official schemes: a third-party tool can add friction, information, and player-signed locks. It cannot replace a licence, a state list, or a bank. Crypto and grey-market sites remain the hole those lists leave open — that is the only honest product claim.
